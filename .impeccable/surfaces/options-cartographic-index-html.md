THESIS: Decennial Energy Maps is a printed national atlas plate of where the grid has room for data centers; the visitor reads coverage the way a surveyor reads a sheet, and routes to the right app from the plate itself.

OWN-WORLD: The USGS quadrangle and National Atlas plate on wash paper: neatline with a graticule piano-key band and degree ticks, coastline water lining, boxed Alaska and Hawaii insets, registration marks, a true scale bar in the collar, aubergine key-plate linework, lilac and aubergine color plates for the tiers, gold overprinted transmission corridors drawn as cased lines with tower ticks. Work Sans at display scale with real weight contrast; no serif, no italics, no tracked labels, no newspaper chrome.

STORY: Megawatts before acres: power is read first, at both voltages. The plate shows which states are covered and at what depth; the inset plates walk the power path from plant to transmission to substation to feeder to data hall and pull back to the national plate; the index plate and gazetteer route to every map.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The headline set as the plate title across the head of the sheet; beneath it the explanation column (subhead, the working Find your state control, the legend) faces the national geographic plate, which fills about two thirds of the sheet with its neatline, collar and scale bar fully in view at 1440x900. Clicking a state or choosing one in Find opens its panel.

FORM: pinned by operator; seed: none (unavailable)
